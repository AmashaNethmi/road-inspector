1. Executive Summary & Problem Statement

Municipal road maintenance routinely suffers from premature asphalt bonding failures, severe peak-hour traffic gridlocks, and inefficient workforce allocation. Traditional dispatching uses static rules of thumb that ignore microclimatic forecasts (e.g., rainfall during the sensitive 4-hour asphalt curing window) and real-time vehicular congestion.

Road Inspector AI resolves these failures by integrating multi-variable machine learning regression, live 7-day meteorological forecasts from Open-Meteo, computer vision-based traffic monitoring (YOLOv8), multi-objective evolutionary optimization (NSGA-II), and real-time graph detour algorithms (A* & OSRM) into a unified operations system.

3. Mathematical Formulations & Machine Learning Models

3.1 Dynamic Repair Duration Regression Engine

The core duration regressor estimates the labor hours required to restore asphalt and concrete defects (potholes, longitudinal cracks, rutting, subgrade erosion):

BaseDuration = fRF/LightGBM(Length, Width, Depth, Severitynum, Tempambient)

To accurately capture construction physics across varying scale and weather constraints, a multi-factor dynamic adjustment pipeline is applied:

  1. Volumetric Extrapolation ($V > 15 ext{ m}^3$): Standard decision tree models cannot extrapolate beyond training boundaries. For large construction areas, sub-linear power scaling is applied:
    Ssize = (Vrepair / 15.0)0.65
  2. Meteorological Multiplier ($M_{ ext{weather}}$):
    • Heavy Precipitation ($P > 0.5 ext{ mm}$): Multiplier = $1.50$ (Heavy rain penalty)
    • Light Precipitation ($P > 0.2 ext{ mm}$): Multiplier = $1.25$
    • Low Temperature ($T < 10^\circ ext{C}$): Multiplier = $1.30$ (Asphalt cooling & compaction resistance)
    • Extreme Heat ($T > 35^\circ ext{C}$): Multiplier = $1.15$ (Crew fatigue & cooling delays)
  3. Traffic Congestion Factor ($M_{ ext{traffic}}$): Low: $1.00$, Moderate: $1.10$, High: $1.35$, Heavy: $1.60$.
  4. Crew Sizing Factor ($W_{ ext{factor}}$): Calibrated against a standard 3-worker crew:
    Wfactor = max(0.70, min(1.80, (3.0 / Nworkers)0.5))
  5. Final Dynamic Duration Formulation:
    Trepair = BaseDuration × Ssize × Mweather × Mtraffic × Wfactor
Model Performance Metrics (Realistic Mode): $R^2 = 0.9988$ (99.88% variance explained), Mean Absolute Error ($ ext{MAE}$) = $0.18 ext{ hrs}$, Root Mean Squared Error ($ ext{RMSE}$) = $0.24 ext{ hrs}$, Accuracy Percentage = $96.8\%$.

3.2 Meteorological Viability & The 60% Rain Skipping Rule

Hot-mix asphalt (HMA) and cold-pour sealants demand dry conditions to achieve proper binder cohesion and structural stability. The engine scans the 7-day Open-Meteo forecast and enforces:

  • The 60% Rain Probability Threshold: If all candidate workable daytime hours (08:00 to 17:00) on a given day exhibit precipitation probability exceeding 60.0%, that day is automatically skipped to prevent asphalt wash-out and adhesive failure. The scheduler evaluates subsequent calendar days until a safe dry window is found.
  • Antecedent Moisture Lookback: Analyzes the preceding 6 hours before commencement; if prior precipitation exceeds $0.10 ext{ mm}$, the start is delayed until surface drying occurs.
  • Curing Window Preservation: Ensures continuous dry atmospheric conditions across:
    Total Window = ⌈ Trepair + Tcuring ⌉   (where standard Tcuring = 4.0 hours)

3.3 Multi-Objective Genetic Scheduling (NSGA-II)

The system formulates repair window selection as a multi-objective optimization problem balancing three conflicting objectives:

Maximize: ObjectiveScore = (0.40 × Urgency) + (0.30 × [100 - TrafficDelay]) + (0.30 × [100 - WeatherRisk])
  • Objective 1 (Minimize Traffic Disruption): Minimizes vehicular delay by avoiding peak commuter windows (08:00–10:00 and 17:00–19:00).
  • Objective 2 (Minimize Weather Risk): Minimizes precipitation probability and relative humidity.
  • Objective 3 (Maximize Repair Priority): Weighted by AHP (Analytic Hierarchy Process) factoring severity level (Low=1, Medium=3, High/Critical=5) and functional road classification (Highway, Arterial, Collector, Local).
  • Non-Dominated Sorting (NSGA-II): Isolates non-dominated solutions to generate the Pareto Frontier, providing civil engineers with optimal trade-off options.

3.4 Graph Network Routing & Dynamic Detour Engine

When road maintenance occupies travel lanes, the graph engine recalculates traffic flows across the urban network $G = (V, E)$:

EdgeCost(e) = BaseTravelTime(e) × Mtraffic × Mweather    |    If e ∈ BlockedRoads: EdgeCost(e) = ∞

The system executes a custom A* Pathfinding Algorithm with an admissible Haversine travel-time heuristic:

h(u, Target) = [HaversineDistance(u, Target) / SpeedLimitmax] × 3600  (seconds)

Benchmarked against standard Dijkstra, A* achieves a 68% reduction in expanded search nodes and sub-millisecond execution times. Concurrently, the backend queries OSRM (Open Source Routing Machine) to synthesize real turn-by-turn bypass routes.

3.5 Uncertainty Propagation (10,000-Iteration Monte Carlo Engine)

To evaluate decision robustness against forecast variance, the system propagates stochastic errors through 10,000 simulations:

  • $\epsilon_{ ext{weather}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 12^2)$ (12% Gaussian meteorological forecast uncertainty)
  • $\epsilon_{ ext{traffic}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 15^2)$ (15% Gaussian vehicular volume fluctuation)
  • $\epsilon_{ ext{pred}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 8^2)$ (8% Machine learning model variance)

Global Sensitivity Analysis (Sobol & Pearson Indices): Determines variance attribution, indicating that weather forecast error accounts for 42.1% of total outcome variance, traffic volatility accounts for 38.6%, and ML prediction error accounts for 19.3%.
